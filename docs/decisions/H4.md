# H4: personal data in the later gaps' framework tables (B18 fix group)

## Summary

B18 (`docs/decisions/B18.md`) exports and erases what a business holds about a person over
contributors. Units that merged later (B8, B10, B17) and A1 left personal data outside it:
calendar connections and invites, the call log, stored integration payloads, acceptance evidence
and one-time codes, and MCP grants. H4 covers them.

- **Calendar** (`features/calendar/privacy.ts`). An export lists the person's calendar
  connections, the records in their calendar, their feeds and the invites sent to them. On
  erasure their connections in the business are deleted with their tokens, event links and the
  inbound connection behind the push channel. Their feeds, pending sign-ins and the invites
  addressed to them (by account or by email) are deleted too. Once the erasure has committed,
  their events leave the Google calendar, the channel closes and the grant is revoked, as a
  disconnect does.
- **Calls** (`features/calls/privacy.ts`). An export lists the calls a person was on as the member
  and the calls with a contact's number. On erasure, a contact's number leaves each call it is on,
  and those calls lose their notes and error. A member is unlinked from their calls and loses
  their own number on them. The calls stay: they are the business's records.
- **Integrations** (`features/integrations/privacy.ts`). This works like audit redaction. When
  records are erased or blanked (by an erasure or by retention), their fields leave the stored
  webhook bodies of their deliveries, the receivers' answers to those deliveries are dropped, and
  the payload of an inbound event whose handler wrote one of them is emptied. On erasure, any
  stored delivery body or inbound payload that holds the person's email or number has that value
  set to null.
- **Acceptance** (`features/acceptance/privacy.ts`). An export lists the person's acceptance
  evidence and their one-time codes. On erasure their codes are deleted. The evidence is kept: it
  is append-only and is the business's record of what was agreed. The erasure counts it under
  `kept`, and the register states it with the application's words for why.
- **MCP grants** (`features/privacy/framework.ts`). On erasure, the person's OAuth consents and
  tokens for the business are deleted, so their MCP clients stop at once. When the account is
  anonymised (the person is in no business any more), every consent and token of theirs goes.
  An export lists their connections.

Mechanisms added to the privacy feature:
- an erasure contributor can return the records it changed (their copies are redacted), what it
  keeps and why, and work to do outside the database after the erasure commits;
- `recordCopies` lets a feature redact its own copies of erased records;
- `personalDataParts` and the policy's `keepReasons` put framework data on the register.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The contributor's result grows** (`ErasureResult` in `hooks.ts`): `count`, plus optional `erased` (records it deleted or blanked, as `ErasedRecords`: their audit, event, approval, outbox and reply copies lose those fields, as declared records' do), `kept` (`[{ name, count, keepReason }]`: data it keeps on purpose, reported in the request's `outcome.kept`) and `afterCommit(ctx)` (work outside the database once the erasure has committed, best effort, logged on failure, never rolled back). `ErasedRecords` moves from the privacy registry to `hooks.ts`, beside the contributor that returns it. | Task (calendar disconnect, evidence kept); B18 decision 14 (copies redacted, rows kept). |
| 2 | **`recordCopies`** (privacy registry): `{ name, redact({ tx, tenantId, set }) → { <where>: count } }`. `redactCopies` runs each one for every erased set, after the framework's own copies, both for erasure and for retention, and adds its counts to `outcome.redacted`. | Task ("matching how audit redaction works"); retention redacts copies too (B18 decision 18). |
| 3 | **Calendar erasure** (a person, in the business): for each connection, the event links and opened tokens are read, then the connection is deleted (event links cascade) along with its B10 inbound connection (its inbound events cascade). Their feeds and pending sign-ins (`calendar_oauth_states`) are deleted. Invites to them (`user_id`, or the account's email, case-insensitive) are deleted, so later changes to the record send them no update and no cancellation. `afterCommit` runs `CalendarSyncService.cleanUp` on each connection whose tokens opened: our events out of their calendar, the channel stopped, the grant revoked. Copies redacted: connections' `account` and `lastError`, the inbound connection's `label` and `public` (since V3: its label names the account), feeds' `label`, invites' `email`, `to` and `name`. A contact has no email: nothing. | Task ("disconnect and delete their calendar connection and tokens"; "anonymise or delete the person's invites"); B17's disconnect (`ops.ts`, `sync.service.ts`). Delete rather than anonymise: an invite row exists only to send to that address. |
| 4 | Invites the person *asked for* (to other people, `requested_by`) stay. They are about other people, and the business still holds the record. | Default: erasing one person never takes another person's invite. |
| 5 | **Calls erasure.** A contact: in the business, each call whose `from_number` or `to_number` is its number (`+<digits>` or the digits) loses that number, its notes and its error. A person: their calls as the member are unlinked (`user_id`, `logged_by` set null) and lose the member's own number: `from` on an outbound call, `to` on an inbound one. Notes on a member's calls stay: they record the business's conversation with someone else. A person's linked WhatsApp numbers are erased as contacts, in the same erasure. Copies redacted: `from`/`to` (the side that was theirs), `notes`, `error`, `userId`. | Task ("anonymise ... their call-log numbers and notes"); B17 decision 24 (E.164 numbers); default for the member side, logged below. |
| 6 | **Integration copies** (`recordCopies` `integrations`), for each erased set: (a) deliveries of those records (`resource_type`/`resource_id`): the fields leave `data.row` and `data.before` of the stored body, and the row, ids and status stay; (b) their attempts lose `response` (the start of the receiver's answer, which may echo the body); (c) inbound events whose handler wrote one of the records (audit rows of the record with `request_id` `inbound-<event id>`, B10's handler context) have `data` emptied (null). The sender's payload has the sender's shape, so the framework cannot tell which of its values are the record's fields. Counted as `redacted.deliveries`, `.attempts`, `.inbound`. | Task; B10 deferred ("erasure of personal data in stored payloads (B18)"); B10 `inbound.service.ts` (`requestId: inbound-<id>`). |
| 7 | **Integrations erasure** (contributor `integrations`): in the business, the stored delivery bodies and inbound payloads that hold the person's account email (case-insensitive) or a contact's number (with or without `+`) as a JSON string have each such value set to null, and those deliveries' attempts lose `response`. Candidates are found in SQL (`position` of the quoted value in the text). Each is then walked in full, so a value that only contains the email or number is left as it is. | Task ("payloads with personal data"); default: exact values only, never a guess. |
| 8 | **Acceptance erasure** (contributor `acceptance`): their one-time codes are deleted: a person's (`user_id`, or `channel = email` to the account's email) and a contact's (`contact_id`, or the number, as the channel's contributor already does). The evidence is kept: `audit.acceptances` is append-only (B8 decision 14) and is the business's record of what was agreed. It is counted in `outcome.kept` as `{ name: 'acceptance.evidence', count, keepReason: 'acceptance_evidence' }` and listed on the register. | Task; B8 decisions 14 and 22. |
| 9 | **Acceptance export**: every evidence row signed by the person (`signer_user_id`) or the contact (`signer_contact_id`) in the business, all its fields (the person's own data: name, where the code went, IP, user agent, device, e-sign certificate), plus their one-time codes (purpose, channel, address, record, times; never the hash). | Task ("Export the person's acceptances"); DPDP right of access. |
| 10 | **MCP grants.** `eraseFramework` deletes the person's `oauth_consent`, `oauth_access_token` and `oauth_refresh_token` rows whose `reference_id` is the business (counted `framework.oauth`). `forgetAccount` deletes all of theirs, in every business, when it anonymises the account. A token is refused at once: `verifyAccessToken` needs its row and the consent. The clients stay, because they belong to no one. Export: `mcp-connections` (client, scopes, when). | Task (A1/B18); A1 `tokens.ts` (consent is the source of truth). |
| 11 | **The register shows framework data.** Features contribute `personalDataParts` (`{ name, label, fields, erase, keepReason? }`), which the register lists under `parts`. The application's policy may give `keepReasons: { <code>: Labels }`, the words for why data is kept. The register shows them as `keepBasis` beside each part's and each entity's `keepReason`. The framework names a code (`acceptance_evidence`), never a legal basis. The sample says why acceptance evidence is kept, and the fixture says it for the test. | Task ("stated in the register"); boundary rule (legal-basis text is the application's). |
| 12 | The parts listed: `calendar.connections` (with tokens and feeds) and `calendar.invites` (delete), `calls.log` (anonymise), `integrations.payloads` (anonymise), `acceptance.codes` (delete), `acceptance.evidence` (keep, `acceptance_evidence`), `mcp.grants` (delete). | Task. |
| 13 | Every count lands in the request's `outcome`: `features.<contributor>` (a total per contributor, as before), `kept`, `redacted.<where>` and `framework.oauth`. | B18 decision 13 (counts are the evidence, never the data). |

## Assumptions to review

| Topic | What we chose | Question for the product owner |
|---|---|---|
| A member's calls | Unlinked; their own number goes; the notes stay | When a team member is forgotten, must the notes they wrote on calls go too, or are they the business's? |
| Which number is the member's | `from` on outbound calls, `to` on inbound ones | Is the member's side always that one, or does the provider's line sit there instead? |
| Invites to the person | Deleted: no update or cancellation reaches them later | Should a cancellation be sent first, so the event leaves their calendar? |
| Acceptance evidence | Kept, named and counted; the application says why | How long must evidence be kept after the person is forgotten, and must it then be anonymised (a retention rule on an append-only table)? |
| Inbound payloads | Emptied when they wrote an erased record; values equal to the email or number nulled | Do any connectors send personal data that matches neither (names, formatted numbers) and so needs a connector-specific redactor? |
| Google clean-up | After the commit, best effort, logged | If revoking the Google grant fails, must someone be told so they can revoke it by hand? |
| Clients a person registered | Kept (they are anonymous: dynamic registration) | Must a client registered by a forgotten person be deleted too? |

## API sketch

```ts
// hooks.ts
interface ErasureResult {
  count: number;
  erased?: readonly ErasedRecords[];
  kept?: readonly { name: string; count: number; keepReason: string }[];
  afterCommit?: (ctx: ServerContext) => Promise<void>;
}
interface ErasureContributor { name: string; erase(a): Promise<ErasureResult> }

// features/privacy/registry.ts
interface RecordCopies {
  name: string;
  redact(a: { tx: Tx; tenantId: string; set: ErasedRecords }): Promise<Record<string, number>>;
}
interface PersonalDataPart {
  name: string; label: Labels; fields: readonly string[];
  erase: 'delete' | 'anonymise' | 'keep'; keepReason?: string;
}
definePrivacyPolicy({ name, respondWithinDays?, review?, keepReasons?: Record<string, Labels> })
contributes: { recordCopies: [...], personalDataParts: [...] }

// GET /api/privacy/register
{ ..., records: [{ ..., keepReason, keepBasis }], parts: [{ name, label, fields, erase, keepReason, keepBasis }] }

// a request's outcome after erasure
{ features: { calendar, calls, integrations, acceptance, ... }, kept: { 'acceptance.evidence': { kept, keepReason } },
  redacted: { ..., deliveries, attempts, inbound }, framework: { ..., oauth } }
// export archive parts: features/calendarConnections, features/calendarEvents, features/calendarFeeds,
// features/calendarInvites, features/calls, features/acceptances, features/otpChallenges, mcp-connections
```

## Test plan

`framework/api/test/framework/privacy.e2e.test.ts`, describe "the later parts":
- A customer with a calendar connection (with its event links, inbound connection and events), a
  feed, a pending sign-in, an invite to their email, calls with their linked number and as nobody's
  member, acceptance evidence and codes, an MCP connection, and a card delivered to a webhook whose
  delivery, attempt and inbound copies hold their fields. A second business holds the same kinds
  of rows for the same email and number.
- Their export has `features/calendarConnections`, `calendarEvents`, `calendarFeeds`,
  `calendarInvites`, `calls`, `acceptances`, `otpChallenges` and `mcp-connections`. No sealed token
  or code hash is in it.
- After erasure the outcome counts each part: `features.calendar`, `features.calls`,
  `features.integrations`, `features.acceptance`, `kept['acceptance.evidence']`,
  `redacted.deliveries`, `.attempts`, `.inbound` and `framework.oauth`. The rows are as decided: the
  connection, feeds, states and invites gone; the call numbers and notes null; the delivery body
  without the card's personal fields; the attempt without a response; the inbound payloads emptied
  or nulled; the codes gone; the evidence still there.
- Their MCP token gets 401 on the REST API.
- The other business's rows are untouched.
- A member's erasure: their calls are unlinked and lose their own number, and the notes stay.
- The register lists the parts, and the fixture's `keepReasons` gives the evidence's `keepBasis`.
- Retention: a card deleted by its rule also loses its fields from its stored delivery.

## Deferred

- Approvals parked by someone else's assistant that name the person's email (an invite waiting for
  approval): the summary and input keep the email until the approval is decided or expires.
- Retention periods for the integration logs, the call log and invites: no targets yet, so they
  are kept until erasure or until their endpoint or connection is deleted.
- An anonymised evidence row after a retention period: the table is append-only.
- A connector-specific redactor for inbound payloads.
