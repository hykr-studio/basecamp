# V8: erasure reaches every feature's copies

## Summary

A fix unit from the live validation of `feat/platform-gaps` (`docs/decisions/VALIDATION.md`,
row 3, the class of C8-2 and C8-3), on the critic's two leads, V8-L0 and V8-L3. V3 made
`redactCopies` find every copy of an erased record at any depth, but only for the record sets
that contributors return in `erased`. Most framework features returned only a count, so after a
person was forgotten their name, email, number and words stayed in the copies those features
write. Redis was never touched, and BullMQ keeps each queue's last 1,000 completed and 5,000
failed jobs, each holding its whole data: an event's envelope with its row and before, or a
notification's row.

The test (`framework/api/test/framework/erasure-copies.e2e.test.ts`) seeds the lead's steps
through the API: a member's comments, a comment naming him, party members by email and by
number, share links naming him and a contact, a business memory, an agent with its run, an
agent run started by the contact's WhatsApp message, a job started for him, another member's
inbox item and push log. It erases the member at his own request and the contact by a team
filing, approved by a second person. It then reads every row of every table in every schema,
and every key under the run's queue prefix in Redis (hashes, lists, sets, sorted sets, streams),
for the markers. On the code before this unit, it found 22 rows and 19 Redis keys:

| Where | What stayed |
|---|---|
| `audit.events` | `comment.create` (the member's words; a mention's name), `party_member.create` (email; the contact's name and number), `party.create` (the contact's person party), `shareLink.create` (`to.email`, the label), `memory.create` (the text), `agent.create` (the instructions), `job.start` (the input), `file.attach` (a job file's name) |
| `app.domain_events` | `comment.added`, `comment.mentioned` (`author.name`, `excerpt`, `target.label`), `memory.created`, `agent.created` |
| Feature rows | `app.party_members` (a pending invitation to the member's email), `app.share_links` (his email; both labels), `app.agent_runs` (the contact's message and name in `input`, the reply), `app.thread_messages` (the run's turn in the agent's thread), `mastra.mastra_scorers` (that run's scores) |
| Redis | completed jobs of `events` (every subscriber's copy of each event), `notify-dispatch`, `notify.events`, `privacy.requests` (its return value named the contact's number), and the `privacy.requests` event stream |

Two more sites showed up once the member mentioned someone: the inbox item in the other
member's inbox (`channel.inbox_items`: his name and the excerpt), and a push ticket's
`meta.row` (`channel.push_tickets`, the record kept for the next channel). On the code after this
unit, the sweep finds nothing. Two more classes showed up as the test grew, each red first: the
copies of what the member had deleted himself before (a comment, a memory, an agent), and the
run of another member's agent that his @-mention started (its input, label, reply, thread turns
and Mastra rows).

A review of that work found two more gaps of the same class, each shown red by the test
before it was fixed. A run of another person's agent whose input carried an erased record (an
event or condition trigger's `record`, a mention run's `comment`) kept it, with its reply, its
thread turns and its Mastra rows, because the agents step matched runs only by the subject's own
ids. And records about the person that were removed or edited before the erasure (a party
member taken off a party, a comment that mentioned them and was deleted or edited) kept their
copies, because contributors found only live rows and the records the person had created
(decisions 16 and 17).

A second review, and V3's review (which handed its erasure leftovers to this unit), found the
same class in more places, each shown red by the test before it was fixed (round 3). A
declared personal record deleted or moved to someone else before the erasure kept its copies,
because `eraseRecords` found only the live rows; so did a page the person saved and deleted, and
an upload they threw away. The approvals a person or a contact asked for were deleted before
`redactCopies` ran, so their copies (the park's row, the stored event, the notification, the
kept reply) kept what was asked, and other people's inbox items asking them to decide stayed,
as did the items about an approval about an erased record. Erasing a person could delete a
person party that someone else had been on. A Mastra table missing a column a statement names
failed the erasure. Decisions 20 to 35.

A third review (round 4) found two more, each shown red by the test before it was fixed. An undo
window (`app.undo_windows`) holds the rows its write touched whole, so a personal record deleted
inside one kept a copy there after the erasure, and undoing the delete put it back, subject
column and all. And round 3's own fix for records moved to someone else emptied a pending
request about such a record (its summary and input), so deciding it failed. A sweep of every
`jsonb` column for the same class found one more: an outside worker's updates to an erased job
(`app.job_updates.body`) kept its output and message. And the other stored write that can put an
erased record back, a request still waiting for a decision, did: Carol's parked request to log a
caller for the contact survived the contact's erasure, and approving it made the caller again
with the erased contact's id and name. Decisions 36 to 45.

Two parts of the lead did not hold. The record-level row of a comment command (`comment.remove`
on the to-do) holds only `{ touched: [{ type, id, change }] }` (`runWrite`, pipeline.ts), and the
comment's own `comment.delete` row is now redacted (decision 14). The members feature has a
contributor since V3 (`invites`), and a membership's copies hold ids and roles only.

## Assumptions to review

- Copies of other people's comments that named the person lose their whole body and mentions
  (decision 2), not only the token. The live comment keeps its rewritten text.
- Inbox items are matched by their notification's key when they link to another record
  (decision 6). An application whose `dedupe(row)` does not name the row leaves such an item
  until its owner clears it; the item links to the record and the record's other copies are
  gone.
- The Redis sweep removes any finished job naming one of the ids, including jobs about
  records that stay and that only name the person (an event of a task they created, with
  `createdBy`). Finished jobs are history, not work; removing more is the safe side.
- A person's carried runs (decision 5) are found by their user id anywhere in a run's input:
  a run of another member's agent on an event the person caused (its `actorId`) loses its input,
  reply and turns too, though the record it was about stays.
- A run of any agent whose input holds an erased or blanked record is forgotten whole (decision
  16), including runs about a record a retention rule anonymised: its reply was made from the
  record, and rewriting it is not worth the risk.
- Inbox items about every erased set are deleted (decision 6), including sets whose records stay
  (comments that named the person, blanked records): broader than needed, on the safe side.
- A person party the person was ever a member of (by the audit rows of that member, decision
  17) that has no member left at the erasure is deleted, as a live one is, only when every member
  its audit rows show was theirs (decision 28). A party only they were ever on is deleted even
  when it was named after someone else (Sam put on Ravi's party by mistake and taken off, Ravi
  never a member): the audit trail cannot tell it from a party that was them. A party with
  members no audit row shows (written by SQL) counts only the ones it shows.
- A record moved to someone else before the erasure (decision 20) keeps its row, files and
  memory, but every copy of it loses the trait's fields, including copies written after the
  move about the new subject: the copies are history, and telling the two apart is not worth the
  risk. Only records whose audit rows name the person by a subject column are found; a subject
  column filled by SQL with no audit row is not.
- A decider's inbox item about an approval about an erased record is deleted even while the
  approval is pending (decision 24): the approval stays in their Approvals list, without its
  summary.
- An undo window that holds an erased record is deleted whole (decision 36), even when its write
  touched other rows too, and even when it holds the record only nested in another's row (any
  depth): that write can no longer be undone. A moved record's window goes too, though the
  record stays: undoing a move would hand it back to the erased person.
- A decided request whose input names them by a subject column stays in the business's
  Approvals list, without its summary and the trait's fields (decision 45); only a waiting one is
  deleted. The match is at the input's top: a command whose input nests the subject
  (`{ brief: { customerId } }`) is not found.
- A request about a moved record still waiting for a decision keeps what was asked (decision
  37), even when it was asked while the record was still theirs (a rename to their name, asked
  before the move). Notifications about a moved record not sent yet (`app.outbox`) lose the
  fields, as every copy does.
- 24 hours and 7 days are defaults for a deployment to change, not business settings: they bound
  an operational cache, not a business's retention.
- Briefs: 365 days from the last change is the sample's choice. A brief the team typed in for
  nobody in particular has no customer, so no subject: neither erasure nor the retention rule
  reaches it (as a note with no customer). A form answer on a link that names nobody files the
  brief as a new customer record with no account or contact, which nobody can file a request
  for: only the retention rule reaches it. So does a link sent to an email (`to: { email }`):
  the form makes a customer named by the answer (or the email) and linked to nobody, so erasing
  the account with that email revokes the link but reaches neither that customer nor the brief.
  Linking such a customer to an account by an email nobody verified is an identity decision of
  the share feature (`share/ops.ts`, `customerFor`), not this unit's; section 14 lists it.

## Deferred

- Text already delivered to other people is not rewritten: a WhatsApp message's or an email's
  text, a push already sent, an inbox item about a record that stays (V3 redacts
  `channel.messages.meta`, not the text). It is what the recipient was told.
- The approval of a parked comment **add** names no comment (V3's Deferred, parked creates):
  once decided, its input keeps the words.
- Jobs in flight that carry an erased record's row (decision 9) are delivered after the
  erasure. Removing the delayed and waiting jobs whose `row.id` is an erased record's id (not
  the sweep's broad match) would close it without dropping other people's reminders.
- A finished job that names the person by none of the ids, emails or numbers (only by a display
  name) is not matched; it goes within the keep bound (decision 10).
- A run of another person's assistant or agent that read an erased record through a tool keeps
  what it read (decision 31): its reply, its turns in that person's thread and its Mastra spans.
  Closing it means finding the runs whose spans hold an erased id (`traceId` is the run) and
  deleting those spans and turns, over every span of the business.
- Erasure's scans (decision 32): gathering each table's candidate rows once per erasure or
  retention run, and an expression index for `targetTraceId`.
- The register row "Mastra's stores" (decision 35a) is for the integrator to apply, and a row
  for `app.undo_windows`: "Undo windows hold the rows their write touched; an erasure or a
  retention rule deletes the windows that hold an erased or blanked record (V8 decision 36)."
- A document sent to an email address keeps it after that account's erasure
  (`app.document_sends.recipient`, its `documentSend.create` audit row; decision 44). Closing it
  needs a documents erasure contributor that rewrites `{ email }` to `{ userId }` for the
  account's emails and returns the sends as a set (`recipient`), against the documents feature's
  decision that documents are the business's.
- Retention reaches only live rows (decision 39): a declared record deleted by hand keeps its
  audit copies until its subject is erased. A retention target could find gone records of its
  type older than the period through their `<type>.delete` audit rows.
- Rendered PDFs stay with their record (`documents/privacy.ts`, by decision); export archives of
  a person's own requests stay under `privacy.archives`, as before.
