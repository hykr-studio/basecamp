# X1: who is outside the business, decided without role names

## Summary

A fix from the completeness and security review. The framework decided two things from role
names, which an application may rename:

- **Who decides a request nobody routed.** `decidersFor` (`framework/core/src/write/approvals.ts`)
  treated a principal whose roles were all the literal `customer` as outside the business, and
  everyone else with a user id as someone who decides their own (assistant's) request. An
  application that gives contacts and participants its own role (the fixture's `fixture_client`,
  the PRD's homeowners) let a participant approve their own assistant's parked write.
- **Who may be a named approver.** A `{ userId }` approver (an amount band's, a rule's) had to hold
  a role other than the literal `customer`: a renamed outsider role, or a role nobody declared,
  passed.
- **Who hears a credits alert.** `CreditsService.managersOf` listed the members whose roles hold
  `credits.manage` from the code's declarations only (`capabilitiesOf`), ignoring the business's
  own roles and its edits of declared ones, so `credits.threshold` went to the wrong people.

After X1 the framework decides the first two from the principal's server-set standing and the
roles the framework hands outsiders (`givenRoles('contact')`, `givenRoles('participant')`), read
through the business's roles. No role name appears in `core`. `credits.threshold` no longer names
recipients: the application's notification addresses the capability, and notify dispatch finds
its holders with the business's roles, as it already did for approvals.

## Decisions

## Assumptions to review

1. **A member who holds only the outsider roles is outside.** A business may make someone a member
   with only the contact's role (the sample's customer accounts are such memberships). We treat them
   as outside: their assistant's requests go to the business, and they cannot be a band's named
   approver. Question: should a membership alone ever make someone a decider of their own requests,
   whatever its roles?
2. **An explicit `by: 'requester'` is honoured for outsiders.** An application that writes
   `by: 'requester'` on a rule that participants can reach lets them approve their own assistant's
   request. Question: should the framework send an outsider's request to the business even when the
   application's rule says `'requester'`?
3. **Alerts go to whoever holds `credits.manage` when they are sent**, not when the cap was reached.
   A role change between the two (seconds, unless the worker is down) moves the alert with it.
   Question: is "current holders" right, or should an alert keep the people of the moment it fired?
4. **A capability decider is not checked for standing.** A request decided by a capability (such as
   `approvals.decide`) may be decided by anyone holding it in the business. A business that gives
   its contact or participant role `approvals.decide` lets those outsiders decide other people's
   requests (support access is refused separately, `support_cannot_decide`). Question: should only
   the team ever decide, whatever a business gives its outsider roles?

## API sketch

```ts
// @hykr/contracts (roles.ts)
/** The roles the framework hands people outside a business's team (contact ∪ participant). */
export function outsiderRoles(): Set<string>;
/** The roles among these that make someone part of the team: those no outsider is given. */
export function teamRoles(names: readonly string[]): string[];

// @hykr/core (write/approvals.ts, internal)
const onTeam = (p: Principal) => p.standing === 'member' && teamRoles(p.roles ?? []).length > 0;
// a named approver: membershipOf(...) → standingIn(member.roles, settings).roles → teamRoles(...)

// @hykr/contracts (settings/credits.ts)
export interface CreditsThresholdRow {
  id; tenantId; cap; level: 'alert' | 'pause'; usedPercent; threshold; periodStart;
}

// the application's notification
to: () => [{ capability: CreditsManageCapability.name }],
```

Removed: `rolesWith` (`@hykr/contracts`), `CreditsThresholdRow.recipients`.

## Test plan

- `framework/policy/src/outsiders.test.ts`: `outsiderRoles` and `teamRoles` follow the
  application's role defaults (the framework's `customer`, a renamed contact role, a participant
  role of its own), and a role name the business lacks makes nobody part of the team.
- `framework/api/test/framework/members.e2e.test.ts` (membersServer, contact → `fixture_client`),
  "who decides for someone outside the team (X1)":
  - a participant's assistant's parked delete is decided by `approvals.decide`, not by the
    participant (who may look, `canDecide: false`, and gets 403 `own_request` on approve); another
    business gets 404 to look or approve; the business approves it and the row goes;
  - a member holding only `fixture_client`: the same; a crew member decides their own;
  - a band's named approver who holds only `fixture_client`, only a role nobody declared, or who is
    a participant gives way to `approvals.decide`; a member with a declared team role or a role the
    business made decides alone, and their approval runs the write;
  - the parked writes are audited (`needs_approval`).
- `framework/api/test/framework/parties.e2e.test.ts`: a participant's parked change (framework
  default roles) names `approvals.decide` as its decider.
- `framework/api/test/framework/credits.e2e.test.ts`: the threshold row carries no recipients; a
  business that made its own role with `credits.manage` and took it from `owner` alerts that
  role's member, not the owner.
- `application/domain/src/modules/account/notifications/account.test.ts`: the alerts address the
  `credits.manage` capability.
