# B7: external participants: parties, participations, businesses per person

## Summary

A business works with people who are not on its team: several people of one client's
household on one record, an outside organisation it reaches only on WhatsApp, a person who is
a member of one business and a participant of another. B7 gives the framework the mechanism;
what kinds of participant there are, and what each may do, is the application's.

- **Parties.** A party is a person or an organisation a business works with
  (`app.parties`, per business). It may belong to a customer record (a household: several
  people of one customer). Its **members** (`app.party_members`) are how the party is reached:
  a WhatsApp contact (by number), or a person with an account, who joins by an emailed link
  (a token, accepted once, by the account with that email).
- **Participations.** A party is put on a record with a **participant role** the application
  declares (`defineParticipantRole`, contributed as `participantRoles`): which kinds of party may
  hold it and which records it applies to. `app.participations` holds (record, party, role).
- **The `participant` grant.** An access rule `{ grant: 'participant', as?, of?, via? }` reaches
  the rows the principal participates in (through any party they are a member of), optionally
  only in some participant roles, or the children of such records (`of` + `via`). It works for a
  person with an account and for a WhatsApp contact.
- **Participants are never members.** A person who is a member of no team in a business but
  is a member of one of its parties works there as a **participant**: the roles the application
  gives participants (`roleDefaults` name `participant`, by default the contact's: `customer`),
  no membership row, and only what the participant grant (and their customer record) reaches.
- **Businesses per person.** `GET /api/me` lists every business the person is in, as member or
  participant, with their roles there. A client sends `x-tenant-id` to work in another one; the
  app's `businessesPlugin` is the switcher. `PUT /api/me/default` sets the business a session
  works in by default.
- **Optional business on sign-up.** `defineServer({ signUp: { createBusiness: false } })`: a
  new account owns no business. Until someone invites them (A4) or adds them to a party, they
  are signed in with no business: `/api/me` says so, and everything else is 403.
- **Channels.** Unknown WhatsApp senders become customers, or, with the business's
  `parties.unknownSenders: 'contact'`, plain contacts with no customer record. Notifications
  can go to a party (`{ partyId }`) or to everyone on a record (`{ participantsOf, as? }`).
  Erasing a contact (or a person) removes their party memberships, and a person party left
  with nobody in it.

The sample: the meetings module declares a `household` participant role (people of the
meeting's customer read the meeting); the orders module declares a `supplier` participant role
(an organisation reads the orders it supplies, without their cost).

## Assumptions to review

- **Who manages parties.** `parties.manage` is owner, admin and ops. Should a staff member who
  owns a record put the client's family on it?
- **Family sees other participants?** Only `parties.read` lists who participates in a record.
  Should a household member see who else is on their project?
- **Named approvers among participants.** The PRD says "only named approvers approve" for family
  access. Here a participant role decides what a person reaches (read or write, per `as`); A5's
  named approvers must be members of the business. Should a participant (a homeowner) decide an
  approval themselves, e.g. accepting a change order?
- **Participant capabilities.** Participants hold the `participant` role default's capabilities
  in every record. Should a participant role carry its own capabilities (a supplier may confirm
  dispatch, a household member may not)?
- **Cross-business parties.** A supplier at two businesses is two parties; KYC is not shared.
  Is a platform-level supplier directory needed (B19 operators)?
- **Unverified email.** Party invitations, like A4's, match the account's email, which sign-up
  does not verify. Fine for now?
- **Unknown senders.** Default stays "every unknown sender is a customer". Should a business
  that works only with outside organisations default to `contact`?

## Test plan

- Fixture e2e (`framework/api/test/framework/parties.e2e.test.ts`, fixture feature
  `src/features/parties/`: a `cases` module, `case` with `caseNote` children and a `budget`
  field hidden by a capability, participant roles `client` (person) and `observer`, and a
  `supplier` organisation role; its own migration set):
  - parties: create, list, get, edit, delete (participations go), capabilities enforced (staff
    403 on manage), another business's party 404, every write audited;
  - members: by number (a contact with no customer record), by email (token once; join with the
    right email; wrong email 403; used, removed, expired 404), another business's 404;
  - participations: a declared role only, kinds and `on` enforced, the record must be reachable,
    another business's record or party 404, idempotent add, remove, deleting the record removes
    them;
  - the grant: a participant (account) reaches only the cases they participate in, in the roles
    `as` allows (read vs write), their children through `via`, creates a child under one but
    not under another, never sees the hidden budget; a WhatsApp contact member reaches the same
    through the assistant; another business's case is 404 even with a participation there;
  - participants are never members: no membership row, `/api/me` lists the business as
    `participant` with its participant roles, `x-tenant-id` works for it and is 403 for a
    business they are not in; members list does not show them;
  - a participant's parked write (the assistant acting for them) is audited;
  - notifications: `{ partyId }` and `{ participantsOf }` reach every joined member, once;
  - unknown senders as `contact`: no customer record;
  - erasure: a contact's memberships go and an emptied person party with them;
  - `PUT /api/me/default` switches the default membership, refuses a business that is not theirs.
- Fixture e2e (`framework/api/test/framework/sign-up.e2e.test.ts`): with
  `signUp: { createBusiness: false }`, a sign-up has no business (`/api/me` with null tenant,
  scoped routes and chat 403 `no_tenant`), joins a party by link and then works there as a
  participant (default business).
- Unit (`framework/policy`): `as` passes through `accessFor`; participant needs a subject.
- Sample (`application/api/test/domain/parties.e2e.test.ts`): household members of a meeting's
  customer read the meeting, not another; a supplier contact reads the order it supplies on
  WhatsApp, without cost; another business 404.

## Deferred

- Agent tools for parties and participants ("add Sita to this meeting").
- A participant as a named approver (A5's approvers are members), and capabilities per
  participant role.
- A back-office Parties screen and a business switcher in the back office.
- Platform-level parties shared across businesses (KYC once), and a shared WhatsApp number
  routing to many businesses (B19).
- Inviting a participant by WhatsApp number to an account (link codes go to numbers in the
  business the person already works in).
- Emailing the party invitation (the inviter shares the link, as A4).
- A WhatsApp contact creating child rows under the participant grant (needs `define-entity.ts`).
