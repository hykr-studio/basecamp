# B18: personal data (DPDP and similar): requests, export, erasure, retention, register

## Summary

A framework feature, `privacy` (`framework/api/src/features/privacy/`), gives every business the
tools a data protection law such as India's DPDP asks of it, with no legal text, period or
processor list of its own:

- **Personal data is declared.** A module marks an entity's personal data with the `privacy`
  trait: which columns tie a record to a person (their account, their customer record, their
  WhatsApp contact), which fields are personal, what erasure does (delete the record, anonymise
  its fields, or keep it for a stated reason) and, optionally, a retention rule (how many days,
  from which date, delete or anonymise).
- **Requests with a deadline.** A person asks for a copy of their data (`export`) or to be
  forgotten (`erase`) in the app, on WhatsApp ("delete my data"), or through the business's staff
  (`privacy.manage`), who log requests received by email or phone. Each is a row in
  `app.privacy_requests` with its intake, its due time (the business's `privacy.respondWithinDays`,
  else the application's policy, else none) and its outcome. A sweeper marks requests past due and
  stores `privacy.request.overdue` for the application's notification.
- **Export and erasure over everything.** `PrivacyService` (the data principal service) runs a
  request in the worker. An export is one zip of JSON files: the account, memberships, contacts,
  consents and messages, threads, approvals asked, the activity audit names them in, assistant
  runs (Mastra traces), each declared entity's records (personal fields only), every export
  contributor's kinds (files, comments, memory, documents, jobs, parties, push, inbox) and earlier
  requests; it is kept on the request. An erasure is one write through `runWrite` as the system
  principal: declared entity records, every erasure contributor (with calendars, calls, the
  integration bus and acceptance, decisions 27 to 29), the framework's own tables (threads,
  pages, links, pending approvals, MCP grants, membership, customer record, contact), the `mastra`
  and `mastra_obs` schemas, then **audit redaction** (personal fields out of `audit.events`,
  `app.domain_events`, `app.approvals`, `app.outbox` and `channel.messages` copies of the records
  it erased) and, when the person is in no business any more, the account itself.
- **Retention.** A daily runner applies every retention rule: the entities' trait rules and the
  framework's named targets (raw WhatsApp payloads, conversation messages, relayed events, relayed
  outbox rows, assistant traces, export archives), each with the period the application declares
  and the business overrides (`privacy.retention`). A target with no period never runs.
- **Register.** `GET /api/privacy/register`: the business as fiduciary (name, grievance contact,
  privacy URL), the processors (the application's, declared with `defineProcessor`, and the
  business's own), every entity's personal data with what erasure does to it, and every retention
  rule with its period in this business.
- **The business's own settings.** `PATCH /api/privacy/settings` (`privacy.configure`, people
  with `privacy.manage`): its deadline, the kinds it reviews, its own retention periods and
  processors, and its grievance contact and privacy notice, audited with the before and after.

The back office gets a Privacy screen (requests, due dates, process, refuse, download, file on
someone's behalf, the business's privacy settings, the register); the app gets `privacyPlugin` ("Your data": a copy, erasure, the
person's requests). The sample's notes declare their personal data and a retention rule; the
application declares its processors, a 30-day answer deadline, 30 days for raw WhatsApp payloads
(the old hard-coded value) and in-app alerts for requests waiting for review or overdue.

## Assumptions to review

| Topic | What we chose | Question for the product owner |
|---|---|---|
| Fiduciary and processor | Each business is the fiduciary of what it holds; requests are per business; the login is erased only when the person is in no business | Is that the lawyer's reading? Must the platform also answer requests for the account itself (a "delete my account" across businesses)? |
| Review | Own requests run at once unless the business reviews that kind; staff-filed ones run at once | Must every erasure be reviewed by a person first? Must a person's identity be checked again (OTP) before an export is released? |
| Deadline | The sample answers within 30 days; no deadline unless declared | What deadline do the rules set (and should it count business days)? Who must hear when it is close, not only when it passed? |
| What erasure deletes | The module decides per entity: delete, anonymise, or keep with a reason | Which record types must survive erasure for tax and legal reasons, for how long, and anonymised how? |
| Membership | Erasure removes the person from the business | Can a current team member be "forgotten" without leaving? |
| Export format | One zip of JSON, personal fields only | Is JSON acceptable, or is a readable (PDF) summary needed? Must files (photos) be included in the copy? |
| Retention | Periods only where declared; the sample keeps raw WhatsApp payloads 30 days, archives 90, notes two years after their last change | What periods apply to leads that never converted, quotes, invoices, photos, chat transcripts, call recordings? |
| Copies we do not reach | Redis job payloads and BullMQ's kept jobs, other people's conversation messages that quoted the person, backups | Is a purge of kept jobs (and a backup expiry policy) needed? |
| Consent | Unchanged (service, reminders, marketing) | Which more purposes, and is a Consent Manager integration expected? |
| Breach log | Not built | Must the platform record personal data breaches and notify the Board and the people affected? |
| Who changes the privacy settings | `privacy.manage` (owners and admins by default), people only, at once (no approval) | Should changing a period or the deadline wait for a second person, or be limited to the owner? |
| The privacy contact | A business that has not set `grievanceContact` shows the framework's placeholder on its register and in WhatsApp's HELP | Must a business name its grievance officer before it goes live (onboarding refuses, or the back office warns)? |
| Shortening a period | A shorter period applies at the next daily run, to data already kept | Must shortening a period wait (a notice period) or ask for a confirmation in the back office? |
| Filings after erasure | A filing about an erased person keeps its kind, intake and their id, without the email, number or note | Is the id enough evidence of whom a request was about, or must the filing keep nothing that links to the person? |
| Acceptance evidence | Kept when the person is forgotten (append-only legal record), listed on the register with the application's reason (H4) | How long after erasure must it be kept, and must it then be anonymised? |

## API sketch

```ts
// contracts: on a spec
traits: {
  privacy: {
    subject: { customer: ['customerId'] },
    fields: ['title', 'body', 'photos'],
    erase: 'delete',
    retention: { name: 'notes.personal', days: 730, from: 'updatedAt', action: 'anonymise' },
    purpose: { en: 'Notes about a customer', hi: '…', te: '…' },
  },
}

// the application
contributes: {
  processors: [defineProcessor({ name: 'Email provider', purpose: { … }, data: ['email'] })],
  privacyPolicies: [definePrivacyPolicy({ name: 'app', respondWithinDays: 30 })],
  retentionPeriods: [{ target: 'channel.inbound-payloads', days: 30 }],
  retentionTargets: [/* the application's own tables */],
}

// a business's settings
privacy: { respondWithinDays?, review: ['erase'], retention: { 'notes.personal': 365 }, processors: [] }

// HTTP
POST /api/privacy/requests                    { kind, for?, intake?, note? }  → WriteResult<PrivacyRequest>
POST /api/privacy/requests/:requestId/process {}                              → WriteResult<PrivacyRequest>
POST /api/privacy/requests/:requestId/refuse  { reason }                      → WriteResult<PrivacyRequest>
PATCH /api/privacy/settings  { respondWithinDays?, review?, retention?, processors?,
                               grievanceContact?, privacyUrl? }               → WriteResult<PrivacyConfiguration>
GET  /api/privacy/settings                    → PrivacyConfiguration          (privacy.manage)
GET  /api/privacy/requests?status=&mine=      → { items: PrivacyRequest[] }
GET  /api/privacy/requests/:id                → PrivacyRequest
GET  /api/privacy/register?lang=              → PrivacyRegister

// events: privacy.request.received | .started | .completed | .refused | .failed | .overdue,
//         privacy.retention.applied
```

## Test plan

- `framework/api/test/framework/privacy.e2e.test.ts` on the fixture's `privacy` feature (a
  `ledgers` module: `card` deleted on erasure with a retention rule, `ledger` kept for a reason,
  `caller` anonymised; a processor, a policy, a level rule that parks processing an erasure):
  - a customer's export: the archive holds their cards' personal fields only, their account, their
    thread, contributors' kinds; not another customer's;
  - another business gets 404 on the request and the archive; a staff member without
    `privacy.manage` sees neither and cannot file for someone (403); a field the business keeps
    (`amountMinor`) is not in the archive;
  - erasure: cards deleted, ledgers kept and counted, callers anonymised, audit rows kept with the
    personal fields gone, events and approvals redacted, thread and membership gone, Mastra rows of
    their runs gone, the account anonymised; the request's outcome counts each part;
  - review: a request waits (`received`), processing an erasure parks for approval (audited
    `needs_approval`), the approval runs it; refusing needs a reason and is audited;
  - deadline: the due time follows the business's setting; a request past due is marked once and
    stores `privacy.request.overdue`;
  - retention: an old card is deleted, a recent one kept, a business's `null` keeps it, the run is
    audited; a framework target runs only with a period;
  - the register lists the business, processors (the application's and the business's), the
    entities' personal data and the periods;
  - an erasure the team files from a letter (the fixture parks intake `post`) by email, and one
    by WhatsApp number: the approval stores the person's id, never the email or number; after the
    erasure no approval, audit row, stored event, notification or kept reply of the business holds
    the email, the number or the note; the `approval.approved` row still names the person by id;
  - the business's own settings: staff without `privacy.manage` and the assistant get 403 on
    reading and changing them, another business's owner reaches nothing, and that business's
    settings stay as they were; the change is audited (`privacy.configure`, `tenant.update` with
    before and after) and stores `settings.changed`; the register, a new request's deadline and
    `runRetention` follow it; a name no rule has, a deployment target, a processor twice and an
    invalid value are 400; null goes back to the defaults.
- The later features (H4, describe "the later features' personal data"): calendars, calls, the
  integration bus, acceptance and MCP grants in the copy and the erasure, counted per part; the
  person's MCP token is refused; another business's rows untouched; a member's calls unlinked;
  the register's parts and keep basis; a delivered record redacted after retention.
- `application/api/test/domain/privacy.e2e.test.ts`: after the sample's team-filed erasure, no
  approval, audit row or kept reply holds the person's email or the team's note.
- The WhatsApp "delete my data" e2e test keeps passing (`whatsapp.e2e.test.ts`).
- Unit tests for the trait checks and for what erasure leaves of a filing (`scrubFiling`), in
  `privacy.test.ts` in `@hykr/api`.

## Deferred

- A filing that waits for an approval (the sample's level rule on the team's erasures) names a
  provisional request id on its approval; the request gets its own id when the approval runs it
  (developer guide section 14).
- A retention period for the replies kept for idempotent retries (`app.idempotency`): nothing
  trims them; erasure and retention redact the records' fields in them.

- Correction and nomination rights, grievance tracking, a breach log.
- Redis: job payloads in BullMQ's kept completed and failed jobs are not purged.
- Other people's conversation messages and tool results that quoted the erased person's records.
- Re-verifying the person (OTP) before an export is released; delivering the archive on WhatsApp
  or by email.
- A consent manager integration and purposes beyond service, reminders and marketing.
