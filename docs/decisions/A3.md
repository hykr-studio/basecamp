# A3: credits, caps and a prepaid wallet

## Summary

Every business's use of the costly things is **metered**: the assistant's tokens and tool calls
(a turn hook), WhatsApp messages sent (the outbound service), and voice seconds (the voice
service). Each use is one row in `app.usage_events`, priced in **credits**, which are minor units
of the deployment's credits currency, and added to a per-period counter.

A business **on credits** (`enforce`) has a budget per period: its `allowance` (credits included
each period) plus its **prepaid wallet** (top-ups and grants, kept in a ledger). Use beyond the
allowance is debited from the wallet. At `alertAt` per cent of the budget (default 80) an alert
event is stored and published; at `pauseAt` (default 100) the business is **paused**: turns,
voice sessions and the message categories it names are refused with a clear reason (402). Each
meter can also have a per-period `limit`, alerted and paused the same way. An operator can
override (run past the cap, or pause) until a time.

Nothing in the framework names a price, an allowance or a plan. Prices and default plans come
from the deployment (`CREDITS_CONFIG`, model prices from A10's `MODEL_PROVIDERS`), and each
business's plan from its `credits` settings section, which only the operator writes (except the
business's own alert threshold). By default everything is metered and nothing is capped.

Buying credits goes through a **purchase provider** hook (`creditPurchaseProviders`): the
framework records the purchase, the provider runs the checkout, and its confirmation tops the
wallet up. No payment gateway is built.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four built-in meters, each a `MeterDef { name, unit, per, label }` in a `meters` registry (contribution key `meters`), so a feature or module can add its own (pages rendered, SMS): `tokens` (per 1,000,000), `tool_calls` (per call), `messages` (per message; the variant is the template's category) and `voice_seconds` (per 60 seconds). Quantities are whole base units. | Gap A3 ("tokens, tool calls, WhatsApp sends and voice minutes"); plan A3 row. Seconds, not minutes, so a short session is not rounded up to a minute; prices are per minute. |
| 2 | A **credit is one minor unit of the deployment's credits currency** (`CREDITS_CONFIG.currency`, ISO 4217). Every cost, allowance, balance and top-up is an integer of it. The framework assumes no currency. | Boundary rule (money is integer minor units plus an ISO currency); gap map owner question ("Unit of credit"): money keeps A10's model prices usable as they are. |
| 3 | `CREDITS_CONFIG` (JSON, read per call and cached by value) holds the deployment's `currency`, `prices` (credits per meter's `per` units, by `meter` or `meter.variant`), `markupPercent` (added to a model's own price) and `defaults` (a plan every business starts from). Malformed JSON or unknown fields stop the API and the worker at startup with every problem listed. Unset: no currency, so uses are metered by quantity at no cost. | Gap A3 ("prices and allowances come from configuration and tenant settings; no plans or prices in code"); A10 decision 3 (fail fast on config). |
| 4 | A token use is priced, in order, by the price for `tokens.<model ref>`, then `tokens`, then the model's own A10 price (`costOf`) plus `markupPercent` when its currency is the credits currency, else nothing (0, logged once per model). | Plan note "price table from A10"; A10 decision 7 ("An unpriced model has no cost; A3 decides"). Default: an operator can price every model without touching `MODEL_PROVIDERS`. |
| 5 | Each business's plan is the settings section `credits` (`contracts/src/settings/credits.ts`): `enforce`, `allowance`, `period` (`day`, `week`, `month`, in the business's time zone), `alertAt`, `pauseAt`, `limits` (per meter, per period, in base units), `prices` (by key, over the deployment's), `pauseMessages` (message variants refused while paused) and `override`. Every field is optional; the effective plan is the framework's neutral defaults, then `CREDITS_CONFIG.defaults`, then the business's own (`limits` and `prices` merge by key). Neutral defaults: not enforced, allowance 0, month, alert 80, pause 100, no limits, no prices, no messages paused. | Gap A3 ("framework defaults are neutral (metering on, caps off until configured)", "default 80% / 100%"); plan ("`TenantSettings: caps and plan`"). |
| 6 | Only the operator writes the plan (`PUT /api/credits/operator/:tenantId/settings`). The business (`credits.manage`, owners and admins by default) writes only its `alertAt` (`PUT /api/credits/alerts`), below its `pauseAt`. A generic settings writer (A11) must leave the `credits` section alone. | Default, fail closed: a business must not set its own allowance or prices. Gap map owner question ("who gets the 80% alert"). |
| 7 | The operator is whoever holds `OPERATOR_API_KEY` (at least 32 characters, unlike the agent keys), sent as `x-operator-key`. Without the variable the operator routes answer 404; a wrong key 401. Operator writes run through `runWrite` as `systemPrincipal({ tenantId, job: 'credits.operator' })`, so they are audited (`actor_kind = system`) and the ledger names the operator's note. B19's operators replace the key later. | Gap A3 ("operator override"); B19 is not built (plan DAG: B19 after A3). |
| 8 | **Budget.** With `enforce`, a period's budget is the allowance plus the wallet: `remaining = max(0, allowance − spent) + balance`, `usedPercent = spent / (spent + remaining)`. Spending first uses the allowance, then debits the wallet (one ledger `debit` per use, after the allowance). The business is paused when `usedPercent ≥ pauseAt` (with nothing left, at once). A business without a currency has no budget, only limits. | Plan A3 ("caps (alert at 80%, pause at 100%) and a prepaid credit wallet"); PRD tab 1 KR ("Runs that exceed their credit cap: Zero (auto-pause at 100%)"). |
| 9 | **Limits.** A meter with a limit pauses at `pauseAt` per cent of it and alerts at `alertAt`, per period, whatever the credits. A limit on `tokens` or `tool_calls` pauses turns; on `voice_seconds`, voice; on `messages`, the variants in `pauseMessages`. | Gap A3 ("caps"); default: the same thresholds for every cap. |
| 10 | Without `enforce`, uses are metered and priced but nothing is debited, alerted or paused. | Gap A3 ("caps off until configured"). |
| 11 | **What pauses.** A paused business's assistant turns (chat and back-office drafts) are refused by the `credits` turn hook before the model runs (`TurnRefused`, 402, nothing saved). New voice sessions are refused (402), and a session's `maxSeconds` is cut to what is left (the voice limit, and the credits at the voice price). Outbound messages of a variant listed in `pauseMessages` are recorded as failed and not sent. Everything else still goes: replies to turns already allowed, sign-in and linking codes, approval requests, handoff notices and staff replies. | Gap map risks ("Pausing at 100% must not block safety-critical sends", "Pausing customer-facing WhatsApp replies … strands homeowners"). Default: no message variant is paused unless configured. |
| 12 | A paused WhatsApp turn from a customer (a contact) opens a handoff (reason `assistant_paused`) so staff see the conversation, and the customer is told a person will reply. A paused member is told the assistant is paused and someone can add credits. | Gap map risk ("A clear refusal plus a handoff is needed"). |
| 13 | Refusal rules (all 402, `{ error: 'turn_refused' \| 'credits_paused', rule, reason }`): `credits_exhausted` (the budget), `usage_limit_reached` (a meter's limit), `credits_suspended` (the operator paused it). | Default: 402 Payment Required says what fixes it. |
| 14 | **Overshoot.** Usage is known after it happens, so one turn (up to 8 steps), one session or one message may take a business past 100%. The wallet may go below zero by that much; the next top-up covers it. Checks and debits are serialized per business (the wallet row is locked), so concurrent uses never double-spend. | Gap map risk ("One turn can overshoot the cap, and concurrent turns race unless the counter is atomic"). |
| 15 | **Operator override**: `{ mode: 'run' \| 'pause', until?, reason }` in the plan. `run` lets everything through (still metered and debited) until `until`; `pause` refuses as if exhausted (`credits_suspended`). An expired override is ignored. Set and cleared by `PUT /api/credits/operator/:tenantId/override` (`null` clears). | Gap A3 ("operator override"). |
| 16 | **Where usage is recorded.** Turns: the `credits` turn hook's `after` records `tokens` (input + output, model as variant, priced as in 4) and `tool_calls` (one per tool the turn called). Messages: when the provider accepts one (`OutboundService.markSent`), so failed sends are free; idempotent per message. Voice: when the worker reports the session's end, idempotent per room; the start audit row now carries the business. The services reach the meter through a slot (`setMetering`, like A10's model gate); with the credits feature off nothing is metered. | Gap map extension points (ChatService.record, OutboundService.queue, VoiceService.start/end). Plan hot-file table (`chat.service.ts` is F5's: turn hooks only). |
| 17 | Turns without a business (Studio, evals with none) are not metered. A turn that failed reports no usage, so only its tool calls are counted. The guardrail's model calls are not counted. | Gap map risk ("Counting tool calls at the API also counts Studio and eval runs"); A10 builds the guardrail per turn without reporting its usage. |
| 18 | **Alerts.** When a use takes a cap (the budget, or a meter's limit) past `alertAt` or `pauseAt`, one `app.credit_alerts` row per business, period, cap and level is written, and the named event `credits.threshold` is stored in the outbox (as `system`/`credits.meter`) and published after commit: `{ id, tenantId, cap, level: 'alert' \| 'pause', usedPercent, threshold, periodStart, recipients }`, where `recipients` are the people whose roles hold `credits.manage`. The application declares the notification that turns it into a message (wording, channels); subscribers (A6, B10) get it from the outbox. A top-up, grant or plan change that takes a cap back under a level deletes that level's row, so it alerts again next time. | Gap A3 ("80% alert schedule"); boundary rule (the wording is the application's). |
| 19 | A schedule (`credits.sweep`, every 10 minutes, queue `credits.alerts`) recomputes every business with use this period or a wallet: it raises alerts a plan change made due (an allowance lowered) without new use, and re-arms levels no longer reached. | Plan A3 ("an 80% alert schedule"); gap map ("a BullMQ job scheduler"). |
| 20 | **Wallet and ledger.** `app.credit_wallets` (one row per business: balance, currency) and `app.credit_ledger` (append-only: `topup`, `grant`, `debit`, with the balance after, a reference and who). A top-up or grant is positive; a debit negative. A wallet's currency never changes: a use priced in another currency is not debited (logged). | Gap A3 ("balance, top-ups, grants, ledger"). |
| 21 | **Purchases.** `POST /api/credits/purchases { amount, provider? }` (`credits.manage`, signed in, people only) goes through `runWrite` (`credits.purchase`: audited, idempotent, parkable by a write hook, replayed by an approval) and records a `requested` purchase. Then the purchase provider's `start()` runs once (`open`, with a checkout URL for the app), and its confirmation, `CreditsService.settlePurchase(id, { status, ref })` (called by the provider's own webhook: with B10 merged, a connector handler (`connectorHandlers`) for the provider's connector calls `ctx.get(CreditsService).settlePurchase(...)`), marks it `paid` (the wallet is topped up through `runWrite` as `credits.topup`, as system) or `failed`. Settling twice does nothing. No provider: 503 `purchases_unavailable`. Several and none named: 400. | Gap A3 ("purchase via a connector hook, no gateway"). |
| 22 | Who sees what (`GET /api/credits`): people with `credits.view` (owner, admin, ops, staff by default) see whether the business is paused and why, the per-cent used and each meter's quantity and limit. Amounts (allowance, spent, balance, remaining, costs, prices, the override) only with `credits.manage`. The ledger and purchases: `credits.manage` only. A customer, the assistant and guests: 403. Another business's purchase: 404. | Default: amounts are financial; staff need to know why the assistant is silent. A10 decision 20 (prices only to managers). |
| 23 | The scripted model reports usage: input tokens are the prompt's characters / 4, output tokens the reply's (at least 1), rounded up. Deterministic, so tests can price turns. | Gap A3 ("Fake model reports non-zero usage"). |
| 24 | **Bookkeeping is not a write.** Usage rows, counters, the debits past the allowance, alert rows and a purchase's `open` status are written by the credits service directly, like the audit trail and the outbox, in one transaction per use. Everything a person or the operator decides (the plan, the override, the alert threshold, grants, purchases) and a provider's settlement (the top-up) go through `runWrite`. The ledger and usage rows can never be updated (a trigger), only removed with their business. | CLAUDE.md rule 1 (one write path for domain writes); default. |
| 25 | **Settings that do not parse fail closed** everywhere: voice and the paused message variants are refused (`credits_unavailable`) and `GET /api/credits` is 503. A turn is refused by the model policy, which reads the same settings (503 `model_policy_invalid`), so the credits hook leaves it to it. Recording never fails: it prices on the deployment's defaults and logs. | Default, fail closed; A10 decision 10. |
| 26 | **The app.** `creditsPlugin` (`@hykr/app`): a `/credits` screen (used per cent per cap, the pause and why, and for managers the balance, buying with the checkout link, and the ledger) and its destination for `credits.view`. A refused turn shows the API's reason in the chat; a refused voice session says the assistant is paused. Amounts are shown in the reader's language with `@hykr/i18n`'s `moneyText`, and a typed amount is read with `@hykr/contracts`' exact `toMinor` (more decimals than the currency has are refused, not rounded), as every other amount since B3B5. `api.credits` in `@hykr/api-client`. | Coverage checklist ("Credits and caps": owner screens); gap A3. |
| 27 | **Sample.** The `account` module words the alert (WhatsApp templates `credits_low_v1` and `credits_out_v1`, email otherwise) for whoever holds `credits.manage`. `.env.example` puts every business on credits: an included monthly allowance, prices per meter, a markup on model prices, marketing paused at 100%. | Boundary rule (prices, plans and wording are the application's). |
| 28 | `@hykr/api` exports `CreditsService` (`status`, `check`, `record`, `settlePurchase`, `grant`), `defineMeter`, `meters`, `creditPurchaseProviders`, `CreditsThreshold` and the op names; it reserves the route segment `credits`. | Default; other features (A1 MCP calls, A6 agent runs, B2 renders) meter through `CreditsService.record` and check with `check`. |

## Assumptions to review

- **A credit is a minor unit of money** in one deployment-wide currency. Should credits be an
  abstract unit with a separate price per credit when buying?
- **One currency per deployment.** A business cannot hold credits in another currency.
- **The operator key** stands in for platform operators until B19. Is one shared key acceptable
  for now, or should operator actions wait for B19?
- **What pauses at 100%**: assistant turns (all channels and drafts), new voice sessions, and the
  message variants the plan lists (none by default; the sample lists `marketing`). Replies,
  codes, approvals, handoffs and staff messages never pause. Should anything else pause (A6
  agent runs will check `CreditsService.check` too)?
- **One turn may overshoot.** The cap is checked before a turn, a session or a message, not in
  the middle of one. Is a bounded overshoot (one action) acceptable, or must tool calls be
  refused mid-turn?
- **Messages are metered when sent**, per message, priced by template category; free-form
  replies inside the 24-hour window are the variant-less `messages` price (set it to 0 if Meta
  does not charge). Should Meta's conversation-based pricing be modelled instead?
- **Voice is metered per business per period** on top of the existing per-person daily limit
  (`VOICE_DAILY_MINUTES`), which stays. Should the per-person limit go?
- **Customers are handed to staff** when the assistant is paused. Is a handoff the right default
  for a paused business's customers?
- **Studio and eval turns** are not metered when they have no business. Evals with a business
  are metered like any turn.
- **Alerts go to the people holding `credits.manage`**, by the application's notification.
  Should the platform operator be told too?
- **Periods** are calendar periods in the business's time zone. Should a period start on the
  business's billing day instead?
- **A failed send is free.** Messages count once the provider accepts them.
- **Top-ups have no expiry** and grants none either. Should granted credits expire at the end of
  a period?
- **A negative balance stays** (an overshoot past the wallet) until a top-up or grant covers it;
  until then it shrinks every period's budget. Should a new period's allowance pay it off instead?
- **Other processes see a plan change within 10 seconds** (the settings cache); the process that
  wrote it at once. Is that acceptable for an operator's pause, or must it be immediate everywhere?
- **The sample puts every business on credits** with an included allowance of 500.00 a month.
  What should the real plans include, and should a new business start on credits at all?

## Test plan

- Unit (`framework/api/src/features/credits/*.test.ts`): config parsing (unset, valid, bad JSON,
  unknown fields, bad currency), the effective plan's merge order, pricing (per variant, the
  generic price, a model's price with markup, another currency), the budget and limit
  arithmetic (allowance first, then the wallet, pause at 100% and at other thresholds, nothing
  left), overrides (run, pause, expired), crossings.
- Unit (`framework/agents`): the scripted model reports non-zero, deterministic usage.
- Unit (`framework/app`): credits as money (minor units per currency, typed amounts).
- Unit (`application/domain`): the sample's alerts (level, recipients, words per language), and
  its templates pass the catalog's checks.
- E2E (`framework/api/test/framework/credits.e2e.test.ts`, fixture `features/credits/`: an alert
  notification by email, an instant purchase provider, a write hook that parks big purchases):
  - metering by default: a turn records tokens and tool calls priced from `CREDITS_CONFIG`;
    nothing is debited or paused; the counters add up.
  - the operator: no key 401, no variable 404; settings audited; another business unaffected.
  - the 80% alert: the `credits.threshold` event is stored once, the email reaches the owner
    (Mailpit), not the staff member.
  - the 100% pause: the next turn is refused (402, nothing saved), voice is refused, a
    marketing message is not sent while a reply is; a WhatsApp customer is handed to staff.
  - limits: a tool-call limit pauses turns; a voice limit cuts `maxSeconds`.
  - the wallet: an instant purchase tops it up and turns run again, the ledger lists top-up and
    debits; a grant; the alert re-arms.
  - overrides: `run` lets a paused business through, `pause` stops a funded one, an expired one
    is ignored.
  - who sees what: staff see no amounts, owners do; customers and the assistant 403; another
    business's purchase 404; a parked purchase is audited and runs on approval.
  - voice: a reported session is metered once per room for the person's business.
